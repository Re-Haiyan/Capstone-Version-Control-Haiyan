## What must be documented by the team (not invented here)

Fill `annotation_process_record_template.md` once per set: the exact model name and version, interface, dates, the complete prompt(s) and system instructions, whether the two sets came from separate sessions, prompts or temperature, how the sheet was filled (copy and paste, script), whether a person reviewed or edited any label and how many, and the file with the raw AI output.

## Consequences for use

1. **Agreement between Team Label 1 and Team Label 2 is descriptive only.** Two outputs of the same AI source (or of near-identical prompts) agreeing says how consistent the AI rubric application is, not how reliable human annotation is, and not that the labels are right. No Cohen's kappa from them may be cited as "inter-annotator reliability".
2. **The 400-review final-test reference is an AI-assisted reference standard, not independently verified ground truth.** A score against it measures agreement with the AI annotator under policy v1. It is reported with that wording. It becomes a verified reference only after a human adjudication of a documented sample (below).
3. **Separation stays absolute.** The frozen 400 reviews, with their labels, are never used for training, validation, calibration, threshold choice, rubric revision or model selection. AI-labelled data for development (the pool and the dev pool) may be used for training and validation, after removing every text that matches a frozen-test review exactly or at cosine 0.9 or more.
4. **Human adjudication.** The 37 disagreements between the two sets (`final_human_evaluation/adjudication_sheet.csv`) are prepared for a human reviewer. Original labels are never overwritten; any decision goes in new columns with the policy version, reviewer and date. Recommended minimum for a defensible reference: a human reviews all disagreements and a random sample (suggested 100) of the agreed rows, and the agreement between that human review and the AI-assisted label is reported.

## Limitations to state in the thesis

AI annotators are consistent but can share systematic biases (for example in how "okay lang", feature lists and generic five-star praise are read); a model trained on AI labels and scored against AI labels will look better than it would against human judgments; neutral is the least reliable class (the sets disagree most there); the rubric changed between the pool and policy v1; and the AI annotator's own error rate against humans is unmeasured until the adjudicated sample exists.
