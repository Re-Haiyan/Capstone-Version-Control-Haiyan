# Proposed policy v2: sentiment versus evidence provenance

**DRAFT — NOT APPROVED, NOT RETROACTIVE.** This document does not alter or supersede `annotation_policy.md` v1 or its SHA lock. It supplies the adviser and team with explicit choices. Do not automatically rewrite any old label, change the scoring reference, or claim consent for a frozen protocol amendment.

## Why the distinction matters

Some Lazada reviews show preselected positive-sounding phrases such as "Great sound quality" with no personal narrative. One AI annotator treated these as neutral no-opinion because no first-person opinion was expressed; another read the overt evaluative words as positive. Without reliable evidence of *how the text entered the review*, neither default can be assumed factually correct. Tag lists may be chosen by buyers, generated by a marketplace, or copied from product descriptions.

The proposed annotation should be multi-dimensional:

| Field | Suggested values | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `expressed_sentiment` | positive, neutral, negative, mixed, unclear | What the review text explicitly conveys, including favorable or unfavorable evaluative adjectives |
| `origin_evidence` | buyer_narrative, buyer_selected_tag_verified, platform_or_listing_template, ambiguous | Whether the text is attributable to a consumer choice/experience; do not infer verified use from mere tag presence |
| `has_personal_use_evidence` | yes, no, uncertain | Whether product performance was personally experienced, not simply hoped for or repeated from a description |
| `service_only` | yes, no | Keep delivery/seller praise separate from product performance |
| `confidence` | high, low | Explicitly flag uncertain S4 and language boundaries |
| `aspects` | evidence spans | Never invent claim features from product title |

## Proposed decision rules

1. Explicit personal evaluation anywhere in the **complete review**, including after a long feature/tag list, takes precedence over an earlier template-like prefix.
2. Pure technical specifications without expressed evaluation are Neutral for text sentiment. The original hidden star rating is never a sentiment label.
3. Overt favorable/unfavorable adjectives can convey text sentiment even without a first-person pronoun, but if the phrase might be autogenerated, set `origin_evidence=ambiguous` and low confidence rather than claiming verified buyer experience.
4. If positively worded features are independently confirmed as buyer-chosen endorsements, they are positive sentiment toward the purchase, not independent proof of real-world product performance.
5. Negative malfunction details, including Filipino/Taglish clauses after a template, are Negative unless a stronger overall positive verdict dominates.
6. Keep delivery-only expressions separate for product conclusions. A positive seller/rider experience does not prove product quality.
7. If explicit positive and negative opinions coexist, apply frozen v1 S1 dominance guidance; never turn mixed into Neutral to fit the model.

## Evaluation and migration

Preserve original v1 files and original metric reports unchanged. Obtain protocol approval before creating any v2 reference labels, and log the exact reviewer/AI origin and evidence. Label a fresh development set under v2, estimate cross-annotator agreement, and compare a *dispute-excluded* training ablation before changing production behavior. Keep final test locked and independent. No existing metric may be retrospectively relabeled as a v2 result.

**Recommend C for disputed training examples until origin is verified**, while keeping textual polarity and provenance as separate reportable dimensions. This is a cautious experimental-data recommendation, not a blanket judgment that all tags are invalid.
