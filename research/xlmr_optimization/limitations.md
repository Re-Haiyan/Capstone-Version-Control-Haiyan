# Limitations and validity threats

1. **No independent human-verified marketplace labels exist.** Every marketplace label (training, validation, historical test) is a team-supplied label of unverified provenance (`research/annotation_provenance.md`): the labels came back minutes after the blank files were created, with no annotator records. A model is trained and scored against them, so every marketplace F1 below measures agreement with those labels, not accuracy. Nothing in this folder has been labelled by a person.
2. **The label sets disagree with each other about "neutral".** The deployed model scores 0.836 (older 150 high-confidence rows) and 0.785 (76 rows) macro F1 on two older team-labelled files and 0.570 on the 846-row historical benchmark; candidates trained on the newer labels gain on the benchmark and lose on the older files (`model_comparison.md`). No candidate can be called better "on marketplace data" without saying which labels, and the choice between them is exactly the unresolved annotation policy (feature lists, lukewarm text, mixed opinions, delivery-only).
3. **The final test does not exist as labelled data.** The frozen 400-review final test and the dev pool are prepared and unlabelled (`annotation/`); the primary target (0.80 macro F1 and 0.80 per class on a fresh, independently human-labelled marketplace test) is therefore **NOT YET VERIFIED**, and the model-selection results below are provisional.
4. **The 846-row `test_v3` is a historical benchmark** (inspected during earlier development; the sentiment recalibrator was evaluated on it). It is reported, never used to choose a candidate here. Selection used a 495-review, 36-product validation split of the team-labelled training pool, which is small: one validation macro F1 has a product-bootstrap interval of several points, and differences between candidates of about 0.01 to 0.02 are within noise.
5. **The deployed fine-tuned FP32 checkpoint, its training script, logs and the slimming report are missing.** The "original" row of the historical table could not be reproduced; a re-fine-tuning of the public base checkpoint on FiReCS (same recipe class) reproduces 0.844 on FiReCS test, which supports but does not prove the historical 0.846. The deployed model has seen FiReCS train (it scores 0.919 on the validation slice cut from it against 0.833 on test), so that slice is not usable for the deployed model.
6. **FiReCS test was used to gate the shipped model** (manifest), so it is not an untouched test for comparisons with the shipped model. Its "neutral" class may include mixed opinions (a statement in the `sentiment-blend` branch's dataset manifest that I did not verify against the FiReCS paper).
7. **Few seeds.** Selected configurations were repeated with additional seeds only where compute allowed (`experiment_log.md`); single-seed differences are not evidence.
8. **Language, template and category fields are heuristics** (stop-word and regular-expression rules, the app's own category classifier); subgroup results are indicative.
9. **Production data are 85% electronics.** The fresh sample cannot cover home, fashion, beauty, food and automotive categories until a new scan is made.
10. **Performance numbers measured on this PC are not the VM's.** The VM measurement (read-only, outside the service) of any candidate artifact must precede a deployment decision.
11. **Domain-adaptive pretraining (Experiment E) was not run**: no evidence that it would help beyond the label problem, a permitted corpus is not established, and a CPU-only masked-language-model pass over about 100,000 reviews would take many hours; it is listed as a later step.
12. **Product-conclusion effects are behaviour changes, not accuracy**: no independently labelled product-level judgments exist.
