# Recommendation policy: live versus candidate

Generated by `recommendation_policy_compare.py` on the 182 saved products. **Nothing here changes a live decision.**

**What this can and cannot say.** It shows what the candidate would change and why. It cannot say whether the change is *right*: there are no independent human conclusion labels yet (the blind sheets in `research/conclusion_eval/` are unfilled). Until annotators supply them, the live policy stays and the candidate is experimental.

## The two policies

See the docstring of `backend/recommendation_policy.py`. In short: the live policy decides every "not positive enough" case from the positive share alone (below 50% is `not_recommended`). The candidate calls a product `not_recommended` only when negative reviews **dominate** (a majority, or at least 35% and more than the positive share), keeps `caution` for hazard concerns that are not backed by negative dominance, and calls the rest `mixed`, or `not_enough` when fewer than 30% of reviews took a side. Hazard overrides (`safety_warning`, unreliable reviews, insufficient evidence) are untouched, and the candidate never promotes a product to `recommended`.

## Recommendation

**Do not adopt yet. Keep the live policy; keep the candidate experimental.** Reasons: (1) there is no independent label to say the changed decisions are better; (2) the thresholds are not validated; (3) one candidate outcome (`not_enough` for mostly-neutral products) needs new card wording because the current label says "Too few reviews".

If the team wants to proceed: have two annotators judge the changed products blind (`research/conclusion_eval/`), compare each policy's agreement with them, and adopt the candidate only if it agrees better and never hides a hazard. The hazard-override tests in `tests/test_recommendation_policy.py` would carry over unchanged.
