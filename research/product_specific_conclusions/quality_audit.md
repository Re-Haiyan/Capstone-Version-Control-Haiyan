## Manual reading (developer; not generated)

The generated conclusions of the 16 real products that had never been read were read by hand, with attention to hair growth, skin and cosmetic effects, food quality
and safety, battery and charging, authenticity, durability and defects. Every automatic rule above had passed, and the reading still found defects, all fixed and kept as tests:

1. An **anti-hair-fall** shampoo was reported as "Many reviewers reported hair fall": at least half of the 17 matched sentences were praise ("less hairfall", "super effective for hair fall", "helping with hair fall").
2. A headline listed "defects" and "faults or failure to work" together, then more reasons.
3. Headlines said "some reviewers report X" even when X had a single report.
4. Typos and contexts read as complaints: "hindu" for "hindi", "for my rashes, I still use steroid cream", "some people may experience irritation", "non greasy", "natuyo" about the product drying.

**No** conclusion claims hair growth, whitening, immunity, muscle gain or any medical or cosmetic benefit (the product titles do; the text does not echo them). Safety and authenticity appear only as
"possible ... not independently verified" and "questions whether the product is genuine". The manual reading is one person's, it covers 16 products, and it is not independent evidence.
